Module 2 — W-3 · locked campaign line

Module 3 — W-4, W-5

Every ministry leader leaves. Almost none of them plan for it.

You may be years from that day or closer than you have told anyone. Either way it is coming — the day someone else unlocks the doors, sits in your chair, and carries what you have carried. What the ministry looks like on that day is being decided now, by whether anyone is willing to have the conversation.

Most ministries put real thought into welcoming a new leader. Far fewer put any into bringing a season to a close. Succession is the thing everyone experiences and almost nobody talks about, and the silence is what costs the ministry — its momentum, its staff, sometimes its donors.

God cares about the ministry more than you ever could, and He sees the full picture. Robin Fuller, Finish Well

Module 4 — W-6

I have sat where you are sitting.

I served in pregnancy center ministry for twenty-three years, sixteen and a half of them at one organization, and I did not know how to leave it. Coming back from a sabbatical, I could finally see what we had built: a ministry that ran on one person. The transition took four years of honest work with a board, a coach, and a God more patient than I was. It was not tidy. It was good.

Since then I have walked alongside other directors through their own transitions. Finish Well is not my story. It is the field guide I did not have.

Robin Fuller, PCC

Professional Certified Coach. She speaks, writes, teaches, and coaches, serving hundreds of ministry leaders each year.

Module 5 — W-7 · signature element

Twelve steps, three movements

Prepare yourself

Steps 1–5

Before a ministry can be ready, its leader has to be. Start the conversation, write down what you actually do, and discern honestly whether this is weariness or release.

Prepare the ministry

Steps 6–9

Strengthen the structure, the hiring, the team, and the board while you are still there to strengthen them.

Hand it off

Steps 10–12

The handoff itself, what a board should look for in a candidate, a word for the one who comes next, and walking away well.

Module 7 — W-9

What you will walk away with

  • Language for a conversation you have been avoiding, and a way to open it without alarming your board or your staff
  • A written record of what you actually do all day, so the ministry is not carrying it in one person’s memory
  • Clarity about timing — the difference between being weary and being released
  • A board prepared to run a process instead of a scramble
  • Documents you can use this month: a staff survey, a project proposal form, and a sample succession plan
  • A plan for your own heart, for the Monday morning after the keys are gone
